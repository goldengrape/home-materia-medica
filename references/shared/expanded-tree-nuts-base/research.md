# Shared Research Parent — Expanded Tree Nuts

> Scope: background shared by the individual macadamia, hazelnut, Brazil nut, pine-kernel, and mixed-nut dossiers. This is not a substitute for product-specific evidence.

## Identity and evidence boundaries

- Tree nuts and edible pine seeds are not one botanical taxon. Record the Latin name, edible plant part, preparation, dose, and comparator for each study.
- A result from one species does not automatically transfer to another. A mixed-nut trial is tied to its tested recipe; a result from an oil or extract is not a whole-kernel result.
- Distinguish replacement from addition. Many controlled feeding studies replace an isocaloric snack or alter the rest of the diet; they do not test adding nuts on top of usual intake.
- Short feeding trials commonly report blood lipids, body composition, glucose, or appetite. These surrogate outcomes do not establish prevention or treatment of clinical disease.
- Composition databases describe the particular food record or sample. Raw, roasted, salted, and sweetened products should not be merged into one category average.

## Safety

Tree nuts are common food allergens. A mixed package can contain several tree nuts, peanuts, seeds, dried fruit, or shared-line ingredients; use the actual ingredient and allergen statements. Brazil nuts are selenium-rich, but natural selenium content varies. NIH Office of Dietary Supplements gives an approximate 68–91 µg per Brazil nut and lists 55 µg/day as the adult recommended dietary allowance and 400 µg/day as the adult tolerable upper intake level for total intake; these are U.S. reference values, not individualized dosing advice.

## Research-level reminder

A review or pooled estimate is useful for locating and summarizing studies, but its result remains limited by the included populations, formulations, comparator foods, duration, and outcome definitions. Preserve null outcomes and avoid converting a biomarker change into a health claim.

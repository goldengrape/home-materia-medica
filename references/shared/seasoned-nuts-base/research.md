# Shared Research Parent — Seasoned Nuts

> Scope: shared category background for salted, honey-roasted, sugar-coated, cocoa-coated, spicy, and mixed-nut products. Product-level recipes remain distinct.

## Product identity and composition

“Seasoned nuts” is a processing family, not a fixed recipe. Coatings may add salt, sugar, honey or syrup, oil, starch, cocoa, chocolate, or spice blends. Read the specific ingredient list and Nutrition Facts for serving size, sodium, total sugars, and energy. A single FoodData Central entry is one food record, not a market survey or category average.

## Directness rules

- Plain nuts do not directly establish the effect of a coated product.
- Peanut trials are adjacent evidence because peanuts are legumes, not tree nuts.
- Studies of cocoa, chocolate, chili, or isolated capsaicin do not test a finished nut snack unless the actual product was studied.
- Processing comparisons must be checked for bundled changes. A roasted-and-salted product compared with raw nuts cannot isolate the effect of sodium.
- Mixed-nut trials represent the formulation tested. Record all ingredients and whether the control was an isocaloric replacement.
- Product composition and clinical effects are different evidence questions. Label data can describe a sample; it does not establish health outcomes.

## Safety

Tree-nut allergens remain relevant after roasting or coating. Coated and mixed products can introduce additional allergens (for example milk, peanuts, seeds, or other nuts) and cross-contact. Users who need to limit sodium or added sugars should assess the label and serving actually eaten rather than relying on a product name such as “honey,” “cocoa,” or “spicy.”
